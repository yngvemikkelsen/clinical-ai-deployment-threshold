Item wording follows Elvidge J, Hawksworth C, Saygin Avsar T, et al. Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards for Interventions That Use Artificial Intelligence (CHEERS-AI). Value Health. 2024;27(9):1196-1205. doi:10.1016/j.jval.2024.05.006

CHEERS-AI comprises the 28 CHEERS 2022 items, 8 of which carry AI-specific elaborations, plus 10 new AI-specific extension items (AI 1 to AI 10). Where an item carries an elaboration, the elaboration is assessed separately from the parent item. Locations refer to the submitted manuscript section headings.

## Title
| No. | Item | AI elaboration | Status | Location |
| 1 | Identify the study as an economic evaluation and specify the interventions being compared. | Indicate that the intervention involves an AI component that is under evaluation. | Partly | Title |
The title identifies clinical artificial intelligence and the decision-analytic deployment problem, but it does not use the term "economic evaluation" or enumerate the three strategies. The strategies and economic-evaluation components are specified in the Abstract and Methods.

## Abstract
| No. | Item | AI elaboration | Status | Location |
| 2 | Provide a structured summary that highlights context, key methods, results, and alternative analysis. | Specify the purpose of the intervention with an AI component, and the AI technique used. | Partly | Abstract |
The abstract states the intervention purpose within the deployment decision problem and identifies the intervention as a retraining-free correction to clinical retrieval embeddings. The specific technique, post hoc zero-phase component analysis whitening, is named in Methods rather than in the abstract, so the elaboration is marked partly reported.

Item 7, AI elaboration (a to h). The AI technique, the locked non-adaptive nature of the correction, its purpose, mechanism, and intended user interaction are reported. The 13 evaluated configurations and the regularisation parameter are identified, but no separate version identifier exists for the intervention. No formal classification by intended purpose or risk tier under the IMDRF Software as a Medical Device or NICE evidence standards frameworks is assigned, and implementation-cost reporting only partly addresses the remaining cost-related detail. The item is therefore marked partly reported. The parent CHEERS 2022 item is fully reported; this partial rating concerns the AI elaboration only.

AI 1, user autonomy. The manuscript states that the intervention informs rather than directs clinical management: the retrieval layer surfaces record content for a clinician who retains the care decision. The adoption parameter represents the probability that retrieved output is acted upon in a decision.

AI 3 and AI 8, learning over time. Not applicable. The intervention is a retraining-free post hoc transformation with fixed parameters, applied to a static embedding space; it does not learn from data collected during use. The CHEERS-AI pilot exercise found no economic evaluation reporting these items, so stating non-applicability explicitly is worthwhile.

AI 4, development of the AI component. Partly, and the gap is the subject of the study rather than an omission in it. For the 13 configurations evaluated, checkpoints and their published sources are identified. For the 55 systems in the extraction frame, only one provided sufficient information for the relevant extraction representation to be inferred; none reported retrieval pooling as such, and training data and bias-identification details were generally unavailable.

AI 7, population differences. Partly. Differences between the corpora used to measure the intervention's effect are characterised in detail, including institution, documentation genre, de-identification regime, and document length. Differences between those corpora and the data used to train the underlying embedding models cannot be characterised, because training corpora are undisclosed for most of the configurations examined. Stated as a limitation.

AI 9. AI-related uncertainty is the principal subject of the study rather than a reporting requirement discharged in passing. The analysis shows that the response-defining state is not reliably recoverable from published descriptions, that response prevalence remains unidentified without explicit classification assumptions, and that uncertainty in response state, screening performance, and relative benefit-harm translation can alter the deployment decision. No monetary value of perfect information is assigned, because the prevalence distribution such a calculation would require is not identified; decision relevance is reported structurally instead.

Item 26, AI elaboration. Findings, limitations, and generalisability are reported at length. The manuscript explicitly distinguishes representational heterogeneity as a deployment risk from algorithmic bias as an equity concern, and states that differential performance or downstream effects across demographic or priority populations were not assessed; fairness and health-equity impacts are therefore not inferred.

AI 10. Implementation requirements are the operational output of the study: the screening design specifies what a site must do before deploying, and the resampling analysis specifies the minimum measurement intensity below which that screen is unreliable.

## Two items that cannot be fully closed
Item 5, study population. The unit of analysis is a deployed retrieval system rather than a patient cohort. Population characteristics are described at the level of the health system whose activity determines deployment scale, and at the level of the document corpora used to measure the technical effect. No patient-level characteristics enter the decision model. This is a genuine mismatch between the study design and what the item assumes, not an omission.

Item AI 7, population differences. Differences between the corpora used to measure the intervention's effect are characterised in detail. Differences between those corpora and the data used to train the underlying embedding models cannot be characterised, because training corpora are undisclosed for most configurations examined. This is the study's own finding rather than a reporting failure, and is stated as a limitation.
