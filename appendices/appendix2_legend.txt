Corpus construction, section extraction, and the transport matrix
================================================================

FILE: appendix2_transport_matrix.csv

One row per configuration x corpus x query format x document variant. Columns:

  Corpus                    er_reason (UCSF) or mimic_discharge (BIDMC)
  Document variant          hpi = presenting-narrative section (primary)
                            full512 = whole note, all configurations capped
                            at 512 tokens (sensitivity)
  Query format              nl = natural language, keyword = keyword
  Configuration             embedding configuration as specified in the panel
  Nominal tier              1 = nominal harm tier, 2 = nominal benefit tier
  Max tokens                sequence length used for that variant
  Baseline MRR@10           before correction
  Corrected MRR@10          after corpus-only whitening at eps = 1e-5
  dMRR@10                   corrected minus baseline
  nominal subgroup          harmed or benefited, from nominal tier
  retains assignment        does the measured sign match the nominal tier
  benchmark response group  harmed or benefited, from the sign of the
                            configuration's mean effect on the derivation
                            corpora; differs from nominal tier for
                            E5-Mistral-7B-ablation
  agrees with benchmark
    response group          does the measured sign match that group

Nominal agreement is 52/52 on section-level text and 47/52 under whole-note
encoding at matched context length. Benchmark-response agreement is 48/52 on
section-level text, the difference being E5-Mistral-7B-ablation, which is harmed
on the benchmark corpora and benefited in all four institutional conditions.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CORPUS CONSTRUCTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Sources         ER-Reason v1.0.0, UCSF emergency-department provider notes
                MIMIC-IV-Note v2.2, BIDMC discharge summaries
                Both PhysioNet credentialed-access; note text is not
                redistributed here.

Sampling        100 documents per corpus, random seed 42, sampled without
                replacement from documents of at least 400 characters.
                No stratification, because the six derivation conditions are
                unstratified and any change in the curve could not otherwise
                be attributed to the corpora rather than to the sampling.

Median length   ER-Reason 6,741 characters; MIMIC-IV-Note 9,774 characters,
                against approximately 400 tokens in the benchmark set.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION EXTRACTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

The presenting narrative was identified by header. Extraction succeeded on
100 of 100 documents in both corpora, but the header vocabulary differs:

  MIMIC-IV-Note   "Brief Hospital Course" or "History of Present Illness"
  ER-Reason       "History of Present Illness" appears in only 4 of 200
                  sampled notes; matching required "HISTORY" with a
                  whitespace boundary

Median extract length: ER-Reason 694 characters, MIMIC-IV-Note 1,269
characters — both length-comparable to the benchmark corpora and encoded
whole by every configuration in the panel.

Residue: 7 of 100 ER-Reason extracts retain a leading form-field label
("Interpreter used?"). This is a corpus property affecting document text only,
never queries, and a constant present in 7 of 100 documents contributes
negligibly to the document covariance on which the transform is fitted.

The same clinical section, differently labelled in two corpora, requiring
different extraction patterns, is a small instance of the paper's own argument
that preprocessing does not transport without local inspection.

----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY A SECTION EXTRACT IS THE PRIMARY ANALYSIS
----------------------------------------------------------------

The panel's maximum sequence length is not uniform and the non-uniformity
aligns with nominal tier: all six nominal harm-tier configurations are
BERT-scale at 512 tokens, while all four decoder configurations, all in the
nominal benefit tier, accept 2,048 or 4,096. Encoding whole notes at native
limits would give one group entire documents and the other a fifth of each,
advantaging the benefit tier for reasons unrelated to whitening. The section
extract is encoded whole by every configuration, so that asymmetry is absent.
The whole-note sensitivity analysis caps all configurations at 512 tokens,
which removes the asymmetry differently and is reported as full512.

Native-limit encoding was not run: it confounds context window with subgroup
and its result would not be interpretable.
